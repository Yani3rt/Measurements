## Overall Impression

The Atelier has a much stronger concept than most local utility apps: it feels personal, editorial, and tied to the physical act of fitting clothes. The biggest opportunity is discipline. Right now the design is beautiful but too ornate for a precision measurement tool. Quiet the supporting UI, make actions more explicit, and let the measurement plate carry the personality.

## What's Working

1. **The measurement plate is memorable and useful.** It gives the app a real artifact, not just a database of numbers.
2. **The local household framing fits the product.** Profiles, offline/service states, and measurement history all support the single-user archive model.
3. **The typography direction has a strong point of view.** Serif numerals and editorial headings separate the app from medical/enterprise software.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The supporting UI is over-decorated for measurement work

**Why it matters:** Users need confidence that measurements are exact. Gradients, glows, rings, oversized radii, nested cards, and decorative modal texture make the interface feel generated and less instrument-like.

**Fix:** Reduce nonessential gradients/glows, tighten radius scale, remove decorative ruler/stripe backgrounds in modals, and reserve visual drama for the technical plate and selected measurement state.

**Suggested command:** `/quieter`

### [P1] Measurement selection overload

**Why it matters:** The front view exposes 10 targets and the torso group exposes 7 items. That pushes users past the four-item working-memory threshold and makes profile completion feel heavier than necessary.

**Fix:** Add progressive disclosure: group by “core fit,” “upper body,” “lower body,” or guide the next missing measurement. Keep the selected/current task dominant and collapse secondary choices.

**Suggested command:** `/distill`

### [P1] The primary action after selecting a measurement is not explicit enough

**Why it matters:** Selecting a measurement reveals icon-heavy edit/history/unit controls. First-time users must infer that pencil means “edit value” and history means “view changes.”

**Fix:** Replace or supplement icon-only actions with text labels: “Edit value,” “View history,” “Show in inches/cm.” Keep labels visible at least in the active measurement panel.

**Suggested command:** `/clarify`

### [P2] Mobile and desktop interaction models diverge

**Why it matters:** On desktop, tapping/selecting a measurement chooses it; on mobile, tapping can start editing. That may be efficient but it makes behavior harder to predict.

**Fix:** Standardize first interaction as select/focus, then use a clear labeled action to edit. If mobile keeps one-tap edit, make the behavior visibly signposted.

**Suggested command:** `/normalize`

### [P2] Confirmation and recovery are under-designed

**Why it matters:** Profile and measurement changes affect a personal archive. The product already has history, but the UI does not fully use it to reassure users after edits or during delete.

**Fix:** Add a subtle save confirmation, replace native delete confirm with an in-system confirmation, and expose “history keeps previous values” reassurance near editing/deleting.

**Suggested command:** `/harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (Power User):** No fast way to jump to waist/neck/etc.; repeated modal editing makes full-profile entry slow; no batch-entry mode; timeline is not optimized as a fast audit surface.

**Jordan (First-Timer):** “Latest stitch,” “fitting card,” and “ledger” create tone but not always clarity; edit/history controls are icon-only; unit toggle requires interpretation; populated workspace has several competing focal points.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User):** Important controls sit high on the screen; many measurements require repeated modal cycles; dense plate callouts may be difficult one-handed; mobile tap behavior may surprise returning users.

## Minor Observations

- Repeated uppercase overlines risk becoming an editorial AI cliché.
- Warm parchment is on-brand but also a saturated AI default; it needs a more specific atelier material cue or more restraint.
- The radius system is too generous in several card/surface contexts.
- Some detector findings may be contextual false positives, but the repeated families still point to real visual density issues.

## Questions to Consider
